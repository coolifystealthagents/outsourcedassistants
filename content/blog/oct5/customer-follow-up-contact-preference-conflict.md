# Resolve conflicting customer contact preferences

A CRM may say phone, a support ticket may say email only, and the customer's latest reply may ask for no calls this week. A Filipino follow-up assistant should not pick whichever instruction is easiest. The safe routine identifies what each record means, pauses the risky outreach, and sends consent or policy questions to the authorized customer owner.

## Identify the communication being proposed

Start with purpose. A service update, requested callback, sales message, payment notice, and emergency operational alert may follow different rules. Record the customer, proposed message, business owner, channel, deadline, source request, and systems holding preferences. Do not treat a general marketing preference as a complete answer for every operational communication.

Distinguish a preference from consent, a temporary request, a legal restriction, a deliverability status, and an internal convenience note. `Prefers phone` does not mean every call is authorized. `Email bounced` does not mean the customer withdrew consent. The assistant can label these records accurately without interpreting legal effect.

Use stable record links rather than copying personal contact details into a project board. Note who created each instruction, when, its scope, and whether the system marks it current. A recent timestamp helps but does not automatically override a formal restriction or a preference covering a different message type.

## Build a conflict view

Place the relevant records side by side. Include channel, wording, purpose, effective dates, source, owner, and system authority. Mark facts that agree and the exact point of conflict. A broad label such as preference mismatch forces the owner to repeat the research.

Define safe defaults in advance. When records disagree about whether outreach is permitted, pause the disputed channel. Continue only actions clearly authorized by policy, such as documenting the pending case or using a customer-requested reply path. Silence, urgency, or a sales deadline should not become permission.

Do not "clean up" the conflict by deleting an older record before the owner decides what it represents. It may be evidence of a valid historical instruction, a system integration problem, or a preference limited to another purpose. Preserve the audit trail and propose a controlled update.

## Work through a three-system example

Suppose the CRM says a customer prefers phone. A support case created last month says email only. The customer's latest email asks, "Please do not call this week; send the replacement status here." A sales manager requests a phone follow-up today because renewal planning is due.

The newest message clearly covers replacement-status communication and a one-week window. It may not decide the separate renewal purpose, but it makes a phone call today risky. The assistant should pause the call, preserve the customer's message, and tell the customer owner which purposes and dates remain unclear.

The owner may decide to reply by email about the replacement, defer renewal contact, or request clarification through the existing thread. The assistant executes the approved choice. They should not call to ask whether calls are allowed, since that uses the disputed channel.

If the owner confirms a preference update, change only the fields and scopes authorized. Record the source, decision owner, effective date, and any expiry. Check whether connected systems synchronize the change or require separate updates. A CRM edit alone is not success if the support platform continues routing calls.

## Separate delivery failures from customer choices

An email bounce, invalid phone number, full voicemail, or blocked text reports a delivery condition. It does not reveal the customer's preferred alternative. Route the failure and use only fallback channels allowed by policy. Do not infer that a failed email authorizes a call.

Likewise, a successful delivery does not prove the customer read or welcomed the message. Track sent, delivered, replied, and preference-confirmed as separate states. If a response is required for a service decision, the business owner defines escalation.

Handle shared contacts carefully. One person may represent several accounts, and one company may list several contacts with different roles. An instruction for a billing contact should not automatically change the technical contact. Use purpose and relationship, not just matching email domains.

## Test conflicts before live delegation

Use synthetic cases: temporary no-call request, marketing opt-out, requested support callback, bounced email, shared household number, two contacts at one company, outdated CRM note, and an urgent internal deadline. Predetermine the permitted actions and decision owners.

Ask the assistant to identify purpose, scope, time window, source authority, and safest next action. Include one case where the older record remains controlling and one where a narrow temporary request changes today's workflow. The exercise should reward correct restraint, not maximum outreach.

Measure conflicts found, disputed contacts paused, preference updates approved, synchronization failures, owner corrections, and outreach sent through an unapproved channel. Review customer complaints separately. A high contact rate is not a quality measure when the question is whether the contact respected the record.

Keep message content minimal and approved. The assistant should not mention internal data conflicts to the customer unless the owner supplies language. A simple confirmation of the chosen channel may be enough. Avoid exposing CRM notes, staff interpretations, or unrelated account information.

## Close the conflict across systems

After an owner decision, update the authoritative record and the systems within scope. Verify the resulting channel, purpose, effective dates, and synchronization. Close obsolete tasks so another assistant does not act on the earlier instruction.

If systems cannot represent the needed scope, preserve a visible stop instruction and route a configuration request. Do not squeeze a temporary, purpose-specific preference into a permanent global field just to clear the queue.

These sources support careful handling of preferences, personal data, and communications risk. They do not determine the legal status of a specific contact, message, jurisdiction, or customer relationship. Qualified legal and compliance owners and the organization's approved policy must resolve those questions.
