# Review candidate rescheduling consistently

Interview rescheduling can look like a simple calendar task while quietly affecting candidate treatment. A Filipino recruitment-scheduling assistant can apply a written rule, preserve the history, and offer approved options. The assistant should not decide that one candidate is unreliable, reject a request, or evaluate an accommodation. Those judgments belong to the authorized recruiter or hiring owner.

## Write the rule before the exception arrives

Define what counts as a reschedule. A candidate asking to move an interview differs from a panel cancellation, a failed meeting link, a scheduling error, or a recruiter changing the interview stage. Keep those causes separate so candidates do not absorb consequences created by the employer.

The rule should state the standard notice window, number of self-service changes if any, approved scheduling horizon, escalation point, no-show handling, panel-caused recovery, and route for accommodation requests. It should also explain how weekends, holidays, and time zones affect deadlines. A phrase such as "reasonable notice" is too vague for consistent administration without examples.

Give the assistant neutral status labels: `candidate requested`, `panel requested`, `system error`, `awaiting recruiter decision`, `new time offered`, `confirmed`, and `closed by recruiter`. Avoid labels such as difficult or unserious. Operational records should describe events, not speculate about character.

## Preserve the sequence, not just the latest invite

For each change, record the interview stage, prior date and time, requester, request time, stated availability, options offered, decision owner, current appointment, and notification evidence. Keep the candidate's own wording only where needed. Personal explanations should not be copied into broad calendar notes or panel channels.

Cancel or update obsolete invitations through the approved platform so two active links do not remain. Preserve the audit history without leaving stale events that confuse the candidate. If the panel changes, verify that new participants receive only the information approved for that interview stage.

Distinguish receipt from agreement. An email marked delivered does not show that the candidate accepted the new time. Use the approved confirmation step and route no-response cases according to the written window. Do not interpret silence as withdrawal unless an authorized policy and recruiter decision support that outcome.

## Work through two second-change requests

Suppose two candidates each request a second reschedule. Candidate A explains that a family emergency changed their availability. Candidate B provides no reason and offers two new windows. At the same time, the panel has already moved Candidate B's interview once because an interviewer became unavailable.

A rule based only on the number of calendar edits would treat both records alike and may count the employer's change against Candidate B. The assistant should reconstruct who initiated each move. Candidate A's second candidate-requested change reaches the policy's recruiter-review point. Candidate B has made only one candidate-requested change; the earlier panel move remains a panel-caused event.

The assistant sends both cases through the correct route using neutral facts. They should not decide that Candidate A's reason is more deserving or demand proof. The recruiter applies the policy, considers any protected or exceptional issue through the appropriate restricted process, and approves the response.

If either candidate mentions a disability, medical need, religious observance, caregiving constraint, or another potentially sensitive circumstance, the assistant should not investigate or place details in the scheduling record. Acknowledge the request with approved language and route it to the designated recruiter or accommodation process.

## Offer options that still satisfy the stage

Approved options should reflect the correct interview length, panel composition, preparation time, sequencing, and decision deadline. A slot is not valid merely because every calendar appears free. Confirm interviewer coverage and any required room or meeting-host account before offering it.

Use one source of available slots. Multiple assistants sending separate lists can lead candidates to select times that were already taken. Put a short expiry on tentative options when needed and state it plainly. If options expire, regenerate them rather than asking the candidate to choose from stale availability.

Keep time-zone confirmation as a separate accuracy check. Show the date, local time, and named zone in candidate-facing communication, especially after a move across a daylight-saving boundary. This supports the schedule but does not decide whether the reschedule request is allowed.

When the panel caused the disruption, the recovery message should own that fact in approved language and avoid implying candidate fault. The hiring owner may prioritize faster replacement options or alternate interviewers. The assistant executes the approved remedy and records its source.

## Test consistency with paired cases

Build synthetic pairs where one relevant fact changes: candidate versus panel request, first versus second request, advance notice versus same-day notice, working link versus platform failure, ordinary scheduling need versus accommodation route, and active stage versus role already closed. Predetermine the correct status and decision owner.

Blind the review when practical so the assistant applies the same fields without being influenced by names or unrelated profile details. Compare the evidence packets and routes, not merely the final appointment. Two candidates can receive different outcomes for documented reasons while the process remains consistent.

Measure correct cause classification, complete history, stale invitations removed, recruiter escalations, candidate confirmations, panel-caused delays, and corrections. Do not use reschedule count as a proxy for candidate quality. Scheduling data describes logistics unless an authorized hiring process establishes a relevant decision.

Audit message tone and privacy. The assistant should not ask why when the rule does not require a reason, promise that a request will be approved, or expose one candidate's circumstances to the panel. Templates should leave space for the actual dates and decision while avoiding mechanical or accusatory language.

## Close the record cleanly

Once a new time is confirmed, link it to the current interview stage, verify recipients and meeting access, and mark earlier invitations obsolete. Preserve the requester and approval evidence according to the recruiting system's retention policy. Remove temporary notes that are not part of the authorized record.

If the recruiter declines a request or closes the candidacy, the recruiter owns that decision and approved wording. The assistant can send or schedule the message only within explicit authority. A calendar workflow should not become an unreviewed rejection mechanism.

Review patterns by cause. Repeated panel changes may signal poor interviewer coverage. Candidate confusion may show unclear time zones or stale links. A high escalation rate may mean the rule lacks practical examples. Fix the process rather than tightening candidate limits to make the queue look cleaner.

These sources support fair hiring administration, appropriate accommodation routing, and privacy-aware data handling. They do not set a universal number of allowed interview changes or decide a candidate's status. The employer's authorized recruiting policy and qualified HR or legal owners govern those decisions.
