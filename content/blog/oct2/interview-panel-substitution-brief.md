# Prepare a brief when an interview panelist changes

Replacing an unavailable interviewer is not only a calendar edit. The original panelist may own a particular assessment, hold information the substitute lacks, or create a conflict that the recruiting team has already considered. A careful substitution preserves the purpose of the interview stage and gives the candidate a clear, accurate update.

An assistant can coordinate the facts and prepare the change. The recruiter or hiring owner should approve the substitute and any change to the assessment.

## Identify what the original panelist owned

Start with the interview plan, not the person's free time. Record the stage, competencies or topics assigned, interview format, duration, question set, scoring method, candidate materials, and decision deadline. Note whether the panelist is a decision-maker, specialist, observer, or interviewer-in-training.

If the plan does not explain why the person is on the panel, ask the recruiting owner. Job title alone does not prove that another person with a similar title can provide the same assessment.

Document the reason for substitution only to the degree needed for scheduling and approval. "Panelist unavailable" is usually enough. Do not copy health, family, personnel, or performance information into the candidate record or calendar.

## Screen the proposed substitute

The recruiting owner should confirm that the substitute can assess the assigned area and use the approved process. Check availability, time zone, required training, system access, relationship to the role, and conflicts of interest under the organization's rules.

Also check prior contact with the candidate. The substitute may have conducted an earlier stage, made a referral, worked with the candidate, or received information that changes the intended independence of the review. The assistant can surface the fact but should not decide whether the conflict is acceptable.

Avoid widening access in advance. Give the approved substitute only the materials needed for the stage, through the authorized system, after approval. Do not forward private candidate messages or download a full applicant file to make briefing easier.

## Preserve the assessment design

Compare the original and replacement plans. The same interview purpose, questions or approved equivalents, time allocation, evidence standard, and scorecard should remain unless the hiring owner authorizes a change.

Suppose a technical interviewer becomes unavailable and an engineering manager can attend. The manager knows the role but was scheduled to conduct the final leadership interview. Moving that person earlier may duplicate later questions and leave the technical exercise without an appropriate reviewer. A calendar match does not solve the design problem. The recruiter may choose another trained interviewer, rearrange both stages, or reschedule.

If a substitute needs a modified question set because of access or expertise, record that as an assessment change. Do not hide it inside the scheduling note.

## Build a concise substitution brief

Give the approver the interview identifier, candidate time zone, current time, proposed substitute, stage purpose, assigned assessment, known conflicts, materials required, briefing time, candidate-notification deadline, and alternatives. State what remains unchanged and what would change.

Offer realistic alternatives. These may include using the approved substitute, swapping responsibilities between existing panelists, moving the session, or keeping the time for a narrower conversation and scheduling the missing assessment separately. The recruiting owner chooses based on the hiring process and candidate impact.

Set a decision deadline early enough to notify the candidate. A last-minute internal change should not become an unexplained delay or surprise introduction.

## Brief without coaching the outcome

Once approved, provide the substitute with the role context, stage purpose, approved questions, scorecard, timing, candidate materials permitted for that stage, accommodations or logistics they need to implement, and the evidence-submission deadline.

Do not summarize another interviewer's opinion as fact or tell the substitute what score to expect. If prior-stage information is intentionally withheld to reduce bias, preserve that boundary. If the process allows the substitute to review earlier evidence, use the recruiting system and record access under the normal rule.

Ask the substitute to confirm receipt and readiness. A calendar acceptance does not show that they read the brief or can use the scorecard.

## Tell the candidate what changed

Use an approved message that names the substitute, role where appropriate, meeting time, format, and whether the interview purpose remains the same. Do not disclose the original panelist's private reason for absence.

If the stage, duration, preparation, or format changes, say so plainly and give the candidate reasonable time to respond. Route accommodation requests, complaints, or concerns about the substitute to the recruiter. The assistant should not decide whether a candidate's concern affects hiring.

Update the invitation, conferencing details, participant permissions, and reminders. Remove the unavailable panelist only after checking whether they still need record access for another authorized purpose.

## Reconcile evidence after the interview

Confirm that the approved substitute attended, used the correct stage record, submitted evidence and scoring through the authorized system, and left no materials in an unapproved location. Record deviations, incomplete scorecards, technical interruptions, and candidate concerns for the recruiting owner.

If the substitute covered only part of the assessment, do not mark the stage complete. Show which evidence is missing and who decides whether another session is required.

Review substitution frequency, notice time, candidate reschedules, unbriefed replacements, duplicated assessment, missing evidence, and owner overrides. Do not evaluate candidates based on administrative disruption. Repeated substitutions may point to weak panel coverage or unrealistic scheduling rather than assistant performance.

Compare candidate treatment across substitutions. Check whether different candidates at the same stage received materially different preparation time, interview length, question coverage, or opportunities to clarify technical problems. The recruiting owner should review any difference that could affect comparability. The assistant records the scheduling facts without ranking candidates or explaining away an inconsistency.

## Sources and limits

The U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission publishes employment guidance, including information related to discrimination and accommodations under U.S. law. The NIST Privacy Framework addresses privacy-risk management, while NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 addresses access and information protection. These sources support careful handling and consistent process design. They do not select interviewers, validate a hiring method, or determine an employment decision. Recruiting, legal, and hiring owners must make those judgments for their organization and jurisdiction.
