# Resolve an expense-category conflict without guessing

A receipt, card feed, and expense policy can describe the same purchase differently. The merchant name may be vague, the card system may assign an automatic category, and the employee may choose another category based on the business purpose. An assistant should not settle that disagreement by choosing whichever label lets the claim submit. The safe job is to assemble the evidence and route the accounting or policy decision to its owner.

## Start with the transaction, not the label

Record the expense identifier, date, merchant, amount, currency, payment source, claimant, business purpose, receipt reference, and the categories in conflict. Keep full card numbers, bank details, and unnecessary personal information out of the review note. Link to the protected source records.

Check whether the records describe one transaction. A restaurant receipt dated late at night may post to the card feed the next day. A hotel invoice can contain room, tax, meals, and deposits. Similar amounts do not prove that two records match.

The merchant category supplied by a payment network is evidence about the merchant classification, not a final statement of business purpose or accounting treatment. Likewise, the category chosen by a claimant is a request until the organization's rule and reviewer accept it.

## Read the active rule

Locate the policy version effective on the transaction date. Record the section, category definition, required evidence, approval limit, exceptions, and owner. Do not rely on a remembered rule or a screenshot with no date.

Some conflicts come from overlapping definitions. A taxi to an airport might fit local transport while a bundled travel policy groups it with airfare and lodging. A subscription used for a client project might look like software or a project cost. The assistant should quote the relevant definitions briefly and ask the policy owner which one governs.

If the policy is silent, say so. Do not convert a previous one-off approval into a standing rule. Prior cases can be included as context only when the reviewer confirms they are comparable.

## Build an evidence hierarchy for the case

Separate source facts from interpretations. The receipt can establish items purchased and the merchant's total. The card feed can establish posted amount, currency, and payment account. The claimant can explain business purpose. The policy defines allowed categories and evidence. A finance reviewer decides how those sources apply.

Suppose a receipt from a hotel includes a room charge, breakfast, parking, and a minibar item. The card feed shows one total under lodging, while policy treats meals and personal items separately. Assigning the full total to lodging hides the line-level conflict. The assistant should itemize the visible charges, identify missing detail, and route any personal or unclear item to the claimant and finance owner.

Never infer tax treatment, deductibility, reimbursement eligibility, or fraud from the category conflict. Those questions require authorized financial or legal judgment.

## Ask a precise clarification

A useful clarification names the missing fact and why it changes processing. "Please provide more information" creates another round of messages. A better request might ask whether the meal was included in the room rate, who attended an approved business meal, or which project code the requester was instructed to use.

Use the approved channel and collect only what the rule requires. Do not ask for medical, family, or other private details to justify a routine expense. When a sensitive exception exists, route it to the designated reviewer without copying the explanation into a broad queue.

Set a response deadline tied to the reporting or reimbursement cycle. While waiting, keep the claim in a visible exception state. Do not submit it under a temporary category unless policy explicitly allows that treatment and identifies who must correct it later.

## Record the review decision

The decision record should show the accepted category, policy basis, reviewer, time, any allocation across categories, and the action taken in the expense system. Preserve the original values and the conflict; do not rewrite the history to make the record appear uncontested.

If the reviewer creates a new interpretation, ask whether the policy owner wants it documented for future cases. A note on one claim may not be visible to the next assistant. Any new rule needs an effective date and approved communication.

When an expense is rejected or returned, use factual wording. State which requirement was not met and who can answer questions. The assistant should not accuse the claimant or speculate about intent.

## Test the routine on mixed cases

Use privacy-safe examples that include a clear category, an automatic card classification that conflicts with purpose, a split hotel bill, a foreign-currency purchase, and an item with missing evidence. Compare how the assistant and finance reviewer identify sources, apply definitions, and stop for judgment.

Review first-pass acceptance, recurring conflict types, claims waiting for owners, corrections after posting, and policy gaps. Separate tool-generated mismatches from claimant errors. A high number of fast submissions can conceal miscoding if nobody samples the evidence.

Foreign-currency claims need a separate check. Preserve the transaction currency, posted currency, conversion date, provider amount, and any fee as distinct fields. The receipt total should not be forced to equal the final card amount when an exchange rate or fee explains the difference. The finance owner decides the approved conversion source and category treatment.

Split allocations also deserve an arithmetic test. Confirm that the allocated lines add back to the original total and that rounding follows the system rule. Link each line to its business-purpose evidence. A balanced allocation can still be wrong if the categories do not match the approved purpose, so arithmetic and policy review remain separate.

Limit access to the claims and fields required for the role. Use individual accounts and multifactor authentication where supported. Define who may edit categories, view payment data, approve claims, export records, and revoke access. Delete temporary working files under the retention rule.

## Sources and limits

The U.S. Government Accountability Office Green Book discusses control activities, quality information, responsibility, and documentation. NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 covers governance, access control, and information protection. The Philippines National Privacy Commission publishes the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and related materials. These sources support controlled handling and review evidence. They do not decide a specific category, reimbursement, tax, or accounting treatment. The authorized finance and policy owners must make those decisions.
