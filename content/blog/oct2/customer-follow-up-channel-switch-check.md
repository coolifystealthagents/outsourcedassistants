# Check before moving a customer follow-up to another channel

A customer may start by email, reply through a portal, ask for a phone call, or send a new message on chat. Moving the conversation can make a response easier, but it can also lose context, expose information, duplicate work, or contact someone through a channel they did not approve. An assistant should not switch channels simply because another one is faster.

The safe task is to confirm identity, permission, purpose, and record continuity. The customer operations owner decides exceptions and commitments.

## Identify why the channel needs to change

Record the current case, channel, customer request, open question, response deadline, and proposed destination. Name the reason for moving. A customer asking for a call is different from an internal team wanting to leave an overloaded inbox. The first may express a preference. The second is an operational convenience that needs an approved rule.

Check whether the current channel still works. A failed email, inaccessible portal, attachment limit, urgent safety concern, or accessibility need may justify another route. Do not label a routine delay as an emergency to bypass controls.

State what the next message must accomplish. If it only schedules a call, it may require little case detail. If it discusses account, payment, health, employment, or security information, stronger identity and privacy controls may apply.

## Confirm the customer's channel choice

Use the organization's recorded preferences and the customer's explicit request. A phone number or social account found in an old record is not automatically approved for the current case. Check the source, last verification, purpose, and any opt-out or suppression state.

Consent can be channel-specific. A customer who accepts service email may not have agreed to text messages or marketing. Keep service follow-up separate from promotional communication. Route questions about legal basis or consent interpretation to the responsible privacy or legal owner.

If the customer requests a channel change inside an authenticated portal, preserve that request. If it arrives from an unfamiliar address or account, verify identity through the approved method before moving sensitive information.

## Carry context without copying everything

Prepare a short handoff that includes the case identifier, confirmed issue, last agreed action, owner, next deadline, and information the recipient needs. Link to the authoritative case record. Do not paste the entire history into a text message, calendar invitation, or broadly visible chat.

Suppose a customer emails about a delivery delay and asks for a phone call. The assistant may offer approved appointment windows and reference the case number. The agent handling the call should open the protected record for addresses, order details, and prior messages. The calendar description should not contain those details.

Now suppose a person sends a direct message on social media claiming to be the same customer and asks for an account update. Similar name and knowledge of the issue are not enough. The assistant should direct the person to the approved verification route without confirming private case facts in public or direct messages.

## Prevent parallel conversations

Mark the case with the active channel, time of change, reason, customer request, and current owner. State whether the old channel remains monitored. If an email reply arrives after the conversation moves to a call, it should return to the same case rather than start an unrelated response.

Avoid sending the same substantive answer through several channels unless an approved process requires it. Duplicated messages can contain different dates or commitments. They can also trigger separate agents to act.

Set a single source of truth. The customer-facing channel carries the conversation, while the case system holds the durable record. After a call, add the approved factual note, decisions, commitments, and next action. Do not record speculation or unnecessary personal detail.

## Check accessibility and timing

A channel can be technically available but unsuitable. Consider the customer's stated accessibility need, language support, time zone, service hours, and ability to retain the information. Do not move a formal decision into a fleeting channel if policy requires a durable notice.

For calls, state the named time zone and who will initiate contact. For chat, explain service hours and what happens if the session ends. For an attachment, check file type, size, access expiry, and whether the destination is authorized to receive it.

If the proposed channel cannot support the required information safely, offer an approved alternative or ask the owner to decide. Convenience does not override confidentiality or accessibility.

## Use a stop rule for consequential requests

Escalate changes involving identity uncertainty, account access, payment, refunds, legal notices, complaints, threats, security incidents, sensitive personal information, accommodation, or a customer promise outside the assistant's authority. Preserve the message and safe interim state.

An assistant may schedule, acknowledge, and route under written rules. They should not promise a resolution, interpret policy, or move a sensitive discussion to a personal account. Never ask staff to use personal phone numbers or messaging profiles unless the organization has explicitly approved and managed that channel.

## Close the channel handoff

After the interaction, confirm that the authoritative case contains the outcome, owner, next date, and any customer commitment. Mark the channel transition complete only when another authorized person can continue from that record.

Review failed contacts, identity escalations, duplicate conversations, stale preferences, missed handoffs, accessibility issues, and corrections to commitments. Separate customer response time from time waiting for an internal decision. Message count does not show whether the case remained coherent.

Test the routine with an email-to-call request, a bounced email, an unknown social account, a portal message with an attachment, and a customer who withdraws permission for one channel. Compare the assistant's action with the customer operations owner's expected boundary.

## Sources and limits

The NIST Digital Identity Guidelines discuss identity proofing and authentication concepts. The NIST Privacy Framework addresses privacy-risk management. The Philippines National Privacy Commission publishes the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and related materials. These sources support careful identity and personal-data handling. They do not decide a company's lawful basis, consent obligations, customer promises, or channel policy. Qualified owners must set those rules for the organization and relevant jurisdictions.
